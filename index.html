What ERA is

ERA has been the student chapter of ISAL since 2016. In practice it is the corner of the conference where a result three weeks old is a perfectly good result, where “I built this and I’m not sure what it means yet” is a legitimate talk, and where asking a senior researcher how they actually got their job is encouraged rather than awkward.

At ALIFE 2026 that happens twice. The Minicon on Sunday is a hybrid day of invited sessions on doing research and surviving it. The Workshop on Tuesday is the emerging researchers themselves — nine lightning talks — followed by the Emerg-ant hackathon.

Who it’s for

  • PhD students and postdocs
  • Undergrads and master’s students
  • Independent and unaffiliated researchers
  • Anyone whose ALife work currently lives in a folder called tmp/

Both days are open to every registered ALIFE 2026 attendee. No separate ticket, no vetting.

    The Emerg-ant Hackathon

    Ants are the standing argument that intelligence does not need a head office. A colony has no manager, no blueprint and no representation of the problem it is solving, and it still finds the shortest route to your kitchen before you’ve finished washing up.

    So: build something ant-shaped. Pheromone fields, foraging and recruitment, stigmergy, brood sorting, bridges made out of bodies, traffic jams that resolve themselves, or a single ant that refuses on principle to cooperate. Simulation, artwork, proof, or something that only works if you don’t look at it directly — all fine.

    Start from nants ↗ or bring your own colony

    nants is a small browser ant sandbox you can fork and have running in about a minute — a floor, some ants, a pheromone field. It exists so nobody spends the session fighting a build system. Starting from scratch, or from your own codebase, is equally welcome. The only rule is that ants are involved somewhere.
